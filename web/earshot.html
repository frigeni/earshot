Earshot

A short message crosses from one device to another as sound, above 16 kHz. No network, no pairing, nothing to point at. The transmitter does not know whether anyone is listening and loops forever: a receiver that joins halfway still gets the whole message.

demo key — no security

The repository ships only the demonstration key. Generate your own, install the same key in the device firmware, and keep operator.json private.

Turn the volume up and hold the devices half a metre apart. The signal sits between 16.4 and 18.8 kHz: nearly inaudible to an adult, audible to children and animals.

Each square is one block of the message. They light up out of order: what arrives is not "block number 7", it is a combination, and the message dissolves out by itself as the combinations simplify.

About 8 bytes per second — the real speed of the ultrasonic band on ordinary phone speakers and microphones: for messages, not files. Open the microphone with echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain turned off; they eat exactly these frequencies. Receiving needs https or localhost.
